# Enterprise AI adoption & strategy

## Summary

The AI Daily Brief consistently frames enterprise AI as an organizational-design
problem more than a technology problem. A stable of named frameworks recurs —
OpenAI's six "[use-case primitives](../glossary.md#use-case-primitive)," Super
Intelligent's Agent Readiness Score and six-dimension "Maturity Maps," Ethan
Mollick's Leadership/Lab/Crowd model — around a dominant empirical claim that most
pilots fail for organizational reasons, and that value is often captured by
individuals ("[shadow AI](../glossary.md#shadow-ai)") rather than organizations.
Multiple 2025–2026 studies report broadly positive ROI (roughly three-quarters or
more), with CEO ownership the strongest single predictor. All figures are
**third-hand** — episode summary notes relaying primary reports — see *Open
questions*.

## Frameworks for finding and choosing use cases

OpenAI's enterprise report distilled 600+ customer use cases into six
department-agnostic primitives — content creation, research, coding, data analysis,
ideation & strategy, and automation — and cited BCG figures that AI leaders grew
revenue 1.5x faster than peers. Nufar Gaspar's agent-readiness framework structures
the work as a four-step portfolio (Identify → Select → Manage → Track) using a
complexity-vs-value matrix, and Super Intelligent's Maturity Maps score readiness
across six dimensions (deployment depth, systems integration, data, outcomes,
people, governance) for ten business functions. Ethan Mollick's "Making AI Work"
argues individual gains don't automatically become organizational gains without
three mechanisms — Leadership (vision), the Lab (experimentation), and the Crowd
(bottom-up "secret cyborg" users).

## Why pilots fail — and the "shadow AI" reframe

The viral MIT "95% of pilots failing" claim is critiqued as methodologically weak
(52 interviews, 150 surveys, ~300 public initiatives); the show argues the real
finding is a **shadow AI economy** — ~90% of employees using LLMs while only ~40%
of companies had bought subscriptions. The host estimates ~80% of pilot failure is
organizational (buy-in, problem-value fit, data readiness) versus ~20% technical.
Across Super Intelligent's 1,000+ audits, **data fragmentation** was the top
blocker and readiness averaged 52.1/100 (see [AI agents](ai-agents.md)).

## ROI is reported positive — and CEO-led

Independent-of-vendor caveats aside, the ROI surveys converge: Wharton GBK (~800
leaders) at 74% positive ROI; the show's own benchmarking study (1,200+ respondents)
at 82% positive, 37% high/transformational, ~8 hours/week saved. KPMG's Q2 2026
data ties returns to accountability — firms with clear AI ownership were 3x more
likely to report ROI, and "meaningful value" jumped 64%→76% in a quarter. A PwC
study found ~75% of AI's gains accrue to the top 20% of firms — an "AI divergence"
(see [competition & geopolitics](ai-competition-and-geopolitics.md)). Counter-evidence
exists: Bain found ~40% of firms below their cost-savings targets, funding the next
wave on "a circular bet with a structural leak."

## Case studies

Norges Bank Investment Management made AI mandatory, scaffolded it with an enabler
team and 40 ambassadors, and reported 20% productivity gains / 213,000 hours saved
in a year. Walmart organized around four "super agents," and Accenture signed to
train 30,000 staff on Claude Code (see [vibe coding](vibe-coding.md)).

## Open questions

- **Third-hand**: owner's notes of episodes relaying primary reports (OpenAI, KPMG,
  Wharton GBK, Bain, PwC, Super Intelligent). Verify against primaries before
  promoting past `draft`/`medium`.
- ROI figures are largely self-reported and drawn from self-selecting, AI-engaged
  respondents; the show flags its own benchmarking study's selection bias.
- Several frameworks come from vendors (Super Intelligent) with a commercial
  interest in the "readiness" framing.
