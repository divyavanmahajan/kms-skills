# State of AI — adoption metrics & trends

## Summary

This page collects the recurring "state of AI" surveys and index reports the AI
Daily Brief synthesized across 2025–2026. The dominant story is accelerating
adoption crossing from experimentation into habitual, value-generating use:
organizational AI usage rose 55%→78% (Stanford AI Index), U.S. paid AI subscriptions
jumped ~25%→~42% in a single quarter, and a majority of American adults now use AI
with ChatGPT leading. Coding emerged as the enterprise "killer app," and Anthropic
overtook OpenAI in enterprise LLM spend share. All statistics are **third-hand** —
episode notes summarizing primary reports (Stanford HAI, Menlo, KPMG, Wharton GBK,
OpenAI/NBER, a16z) — see *Open questions*.

## Organizational and investment trends

Stanford's 2025 AI Index reported organizational AI usage rising from 55% (2023) to
78% (2024), with U.S. private AI investment of $109B (nearly 12x China's $9.3B), and
a stark optimism gap (China 83%, Indonesia 80% vs. US 39%). Paid AI subscriptions
among U.S. businesses jumped ~25%→~42% in a quarter (Ramp), and a persistent
leader-employee perception gap recurs (75% of executives vs. 45% of employees
believing adoption succeeded). Agent pilots and deployments climbed steeply (see
[AI agents](ai-agents.md)) and ROI surveys converged around three-quarters positive
(see [enterprise adoption](enterprise-ai-adoption.md)).

## Consumer adoption and tool landscape

Menlo Ventures (n=5,000) found 61% of American adults used AI in six months (20%
daily); ChatGPT led assistant usage at 28% (Gemini 23%, Meta AI 18%, Alexa 18%, Siri
16%). Parents emerged as power users (79% vs. 54% of non-parents). In a16z's Top 100
Gen AI Consumer Apps, Gemini drew ~12% of ChatGPT's web visits and 22 of 50 mobile
apps were built in China. An OpenAI/NBER study of 1.5M ChatGPT conversations split
usage into Asking 49% / Doing 40% / Expressing 11%, and found the identifiable-user
gender mix shifting toward parity (37%→52% feminine names).

## Where the market is heading

By late 2025, Anthropic's share of enterprise LLM spend rose 12%→24%→40% (2023–2025)
while OpenAI's fell 50%→27%, with Anthropic holding 54% of the AI-coding market — a
thread continued in [competition & geopolitics](ai-competition-and-geopolitics.md).
Coding was ~55% (~$4B) of departmental AI spend, and the year's defining market event
was DeepSeek R1's January 2025 release triggering a $593B single-day NVIDIA drop.
