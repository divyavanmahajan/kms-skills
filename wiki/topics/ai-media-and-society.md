# AI in media, creativity & society

## Summary

The AI Daily Brief frames 2025–2026 as the period when AI-generated media moved from
novelty to a pervasive cultural and commercial force — and provoked an equal-and-
opposite backlash. Google's Veo 3 (native audio+video) and OpenAI's Sora 2 (with a
TikTok-style app and likeness "Cameos") are treated as inflection points that seeded
viral video genres and enabled national-TV AI advertising; Google's "Nano Banana"
image models crossed a professional editing threshold. Running alongside is a strong
counter-current: "[AI slop](../glossary.md#ai-slop)" backlash, copyright suits,
consent controversies, education/cognition worries, and — by 2026 — AI as an overt
political/culture-war issue. All figures are **third-hand** episode notes, several
already hedged in the source — see *Open questions*.

## Generative media crosses commercial thresholds

Veo 3 is repeatedly named the video inflection point — integrated into YouTube
Shorts (~200B views/day) and seeding fan-IP channels (e.g. "Vlogwarts," ~115k
followers from 10 videos in ~12 days). PJ Ace's fully AI-generated Kalshi commercial
aired on network TV during the NBA Finals at an estimated 95% cost reduction — with
the caveat that "brands still pay a premium for taste." In images, Google's "Nano
Banana" (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image, ~4¢/image) and Nano Banana Pro delivered
step-changes in controllable editing, in-image text, and "visual compression"
(turning a 92-page PDF into a summarized whiteboard). Sora 2 solved character
consistency while triggering a "brain rot" backlash.

## The slop, consent, and copyright backlash

Mass-scale cheap generation became a case study in "slop": a suspected-AI band, The
Velvet Sundown, reached 300k–634k monthly Spotify listeners (yet only ~11k
followers), and Inception Point AI runs 3,000+ AI podcast episodes/week at ~$1 each.
Copyright is a central fault line — Disney and Universal sued Midjourney — and
consent flared when YouTube applied undisclosed ML processing to creators' Shorts;
Netflix responded with a red/yellow/green "traffic-light" Gen AI governance framework.

## Society, cognition, and the anti-AI turn

An education episode weighed a small, non-peer-reviewed MIT EEG study (AI users
showing up to 55% lower brain connectivity) against the vocational value of AI
skills. OpenAI's Jan 2026 move to put ads in ChatGPT's free tier (only ~5% of users
convert to paid) was read as commercially inevitable but a trust risk. By early 2026
the notes describe a measurable anti-AI movement — Pew data placing the US last on
AI enthusiasm (10% more excited than concerned) — with the recurring thesis that
public anger targets algorithmic recommendation systems and social-media
disillusionment as much as AI generation itself.

## Open questions

- **Third-hand and often hedged in-source**: the Velvet Sundown's AI origin is
  "suspected," the MIT EEG study is "small, non-peer-reviewed." Preserve those
  caveats; verify before promoting past `draft`/`medium`.
- Adoption/reach figures (Spotify listeners, Shorts views, episode counts) are
  as-reported and not independently audited.
- Sentiment findings (Pew, YouGov) are cited selectively within argumentative
  episodes.
